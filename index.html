AI Ethics

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

Fairness, Trust, Abuse/Misuse (Dual-Use Technology) AI can cause significant harm even when it’s working perfectly fine. We went through three cases showing how AI can affect fairness, trust and how it can be misused. The first case being fairness, we looked at a resume checker and in this case fairness was flawed as it took in biased training data and skewed job opportunities away from people of a certain race, people with disabilities and people of old age, worse these tools didn’t directly ask for any of these characteristics but assumed them based on proxy variables, this is known as Algorithmic Bias which creates disparate impact which is basically when a tool harms a specific group of people (a protected group). The second case was trust as a an AI system was used as a symptom checker and it increasing got less accurate due to the user biases and context drift which is when information from earlier in the conversation becomes less influential as newer messages are added. Calibrated trust in AI is the practice of matching a user's confidence directly to the system's actual competence, limits, and risk level. Lastly we looked at AI misuse, where the creator of the AI system initially intended for the system to be used for good will but in reality people used it in order to manipulate the vulnerable and gain from that. This case focused on a voice cloner, where people used the voice cloning app to fool grandparents that their grandchildren were asking for money and instead they receive it after taking advantage of people’s naiveness. This situation could easily be prevented by the use of a safe word, a private code which a scammer’s clone can not reproduce without previously being aware of the word, this targets verification over recognition. I will add more about automation bias, informed consent, who is responsible in these cases.
